# Never Fabricate User Evidence

Never invent what users said, did, or scored. No made-up quotes, personas, participants, task results, completion rates, or satisfaction scores. A simulated or AI "user" is not a user, and its output is not user evidence.

- **Label the tier.** Every statement about users carries its evidence tier. Inspection is your hypothesis. Existing evidence names its source. New user evidence names its study.
- **Assumptions are titled as assumptions.** A persona, journey, or opportunity map built without research inputs is an assumption map. Call it that in the artifact.
- **Inspection predicts. It does not show.** Heuristic review and walkthrough findings say where users will likely struggle. Testing with users is still required to say they do.
- **Record the gap and keep working.** When a decision needs user evidence you lack, add `pending: user evidence for <question>` to the todo list, prepare the study kit, and continue with what inspection can settle.
- **Cite existing evidence as found.** Do not stretch one small interview round into a claim about all users. Do not round a guess into a number.
- **Mark sample content in the artifact.** Sample names, quotes, and data in a prototype or kit are labeled in the artifact itself. One artifact-level "Sample data" banner can stand in for per-item tags, so realistic content keeps its real length.

**Why:** A fabricated finding looks exactly like a real one downstream. Teams build on it, and nobody can tell where the evidence ran out. A labeled gap is cheap to fill. A fake fact is expensive to find.

## Misuse caveats

- This is not a reason to stop. Missing evidence becomes a gate in the todo list, not a block on the session.
- A cognitive walkthrough you run as the evaluator is tier 1. Never role-play a user to produce quotes, ratings, task results, or preferences, even labeled as simulated.

## Grounded in

- [ISO 9241-210:2019](https://www.iso.org/standard/77520.html) (ISO, all rights reserved, cite clause names only), 7.5.4 user-based testing and 7.5.5 inspection-based evaluation as distinct routes.
- [How to Conduct a Heuristic Evaluation](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-to-conduct-a-heuristic-evaluation/) (NN/g, all rights reserved), heuristic evaluation complements user research and cannot replace it.
- [Opportunity Solution Trees](https://www.producttalk.org/opportunity-solution-trees/) (Teresa Torres, Product Talk, no open license), the tree needs real story-based interviews as input, and invented opportunities empty it of meaning.
- [GOV.UK Service Manual, user research](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research) (Crown copyright, OGL v3), research as planned rounds with consent and participant data handling.
