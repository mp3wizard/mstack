# Observe Behavior Over Opinion

Conclusions come from what users did on realistic tasks. What anyone says they like is a weaker signal. That includes users, stakeholders, and you.

- **Success first.** Record whether each participant finished the task, how, and where they got stuck. Collect ease ratings afterward as a separate signal.
- **Think aloud.** Participants narrate while they work. The facilitator stays neutral, does not lead, and turns questions back to see how people cope alone.
- **Tasks that do not give it away.** Give each task a clear, believable goal, hard enough to expose problems. Never name the control or the label under test.
- **No preference questions.** "Do you like it" and "would you use this" collect opinions, not behavior. Drop them from scripts.
- **Ask about the last time.** In interviews, ask for a specific past instance instead of hypotheticals or wish lists. For a purchase or a switch, rebuild the real timeline from first thought to first use. Generative questions go to **Interview study** (../design-mode/playbooks/interview-study.md). Usability tests stay evaluative.
- **Probe behind praise.** Polished prototypes inflate how easy people say a design is. Ask what they would do next and watch them do it.
- **Stakeholder taste frames the problem.** It is input to framing and priorities. It is not evidence of usability.
- **When behavior and opinion disagree, behavior wins.** The gap itself is a finding. Report both.

**Why:** Opinions are cheap to collect and easy to steer. Behavior on a realistic task shows where the design actually fails.

## Grounded in

- [Thinking aloud](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/thinking-aloud-the-1-usability-tool/) (NN/g, all rights reserved), continuous narration during real tasks with a neutral facilitator.
- [Steve Krug's test kit](https://sensible.com/download-files/) (Steve Krug, share with notice intact, never repost), a fixed facilitator script that tests the product rather than the person and deflects questions.
- [GOV.UK Service Manual, user research](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research) (Crown copyright, OGL v3), tasks with clear, realistic goals that do not hint at the solution.
- [Continuous discovery](https://www.producttalk.org/2021/08/continuous-discovery/) (Teresa Torres, Product Talk, no open license), story-based interviews about a specific past instance.
- [Jobs to Be Done](https://www.christenseninstitute.org/theory/jobs-to-be-done/) (Christensen Institute, all rights reserved), jobs as progress in a circumstance.
- [Aesthetic-usability effect](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/aesthetic-usability-effect/) (NN/g, all rights reserved), attractive prototypes mask problems in tests.
