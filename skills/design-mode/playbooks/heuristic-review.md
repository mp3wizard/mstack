### Heuristic review

**You own the inspection, not the usability verdict. Every finding is a hypothesis until users confirm it.**

Tier 1 evidence. It complements testing with users and never replaces it (**principle-never-fabricate-user-evidence**). For a broader review with content and ethics lenses, run the **critique** skill instead.

1. Scope it. Name the users, their top tasks, and the screens and states in scope. Capture each state on the real surface (the built-in browser or Playwright MCP, the run skill to launch the app) before judging it (**principle-prove-it-with-evidence**).
2. Run independent lens passes, one Agent call per lens (`subagent_type: "mstack:design-agent"`), all launched in one message. Lenses are [Nielsen's 10 heuristics](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/), the seven ISO 9241-110:2020 interaction principles, Shneiderman's eight golden rules, and Norman's two gulfs (can users tell what to do, can they tell what happened). Run the Nielsen lens through impeccable:impeccable critique if installed, and check its output against the rendered UI. Its scores are evidence, not proof. Put one lens on the codex-bridge:codex-second-opinion agent, if installed, for a second model family. Lenses share nothing until consolidation.
3. For a new or complex flow, run a [cognitive walkthrough](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/cognitive-walkthroughs/) yourself as the evaluator. That is tier 1. Never role-play a user to answer it. At each step ask whether users would try for this step at all (CW-1), notice the right control (CW-2), link it to their goal (CW-3), and see that it worked (CW-4). One no fails the step.
4. Each finding cites its IDs from ../critique/references/lenses.md (H1 to H10, ISO110-1 to ISO110-7, SHN-1 to SHN-8, CW-1 to CW-4). Name a Norman finding as the gulf of execution or of evaluation. Each finding also names the screen and state, and evidence (screenshot path, DOM or accessibility-tree excerpt, Figma node ID). No evidence, no finding (**principle-cite-the-law-in-scope**).
5. Consolidate. Merge duplicates across lenses, keep every lens ID a finding hits, and record where lenses disagree. A violation can be a deliberate tradeoff. Say which, and what research would settle it.
6. Rate severity after the hunt, in a fresh round. Spawn new lens agents, give each the consolidated list, and have each rate every finding alone on the 0 to 4 usability scale in ../design-handbook/references/heuristics.md. Report the spread, and label the mean "model-panel mean, not independent raters". Mark any frequency judgment "estimated, no tier 2 data" unless analytics are cited. Where severity decides the next step (3 or 4 goes to a test), record the finding as an open decision for a human designer to rate (**principle-explain-the-metric**).
7. Label every finding a hypothesis. Never state a coverage percentage. Send severity 3 and 4 findings to a **Usability test** (playbooks/usability-test.md) and confirmed causes to **UX defect fix** (playbooks/ux-defect-fix.md).
8. Write the report in the project. Pause before sharing the report outside the session (see Autonomy in the router).

**Reply:** the scope and the report path. Then findings ranked by model-panel mean severity with the spread, each with lens IDs, evidence path, and the hypothesis label. Add where lenses disagreed, what a usability test must confirm, and which findings wait on a human rating.

**Grounded in:** [NN/g: How to conduct a heuristic evaluation](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-to-conduct-a-heuristic-evaluation/), [NN/g: Severity ratings](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-to-rate-the-severity-of-usability-problems/), [ISO 9241-110:2020](https://www.iso.org/standard/75258.html), [Shneiderman: Eight golden rules](https://www.cs.umd.edu/users/ben/goldenrules.html), [Norman: The Design of Everyday Things](https://jnd.org/books/the-design-of-everyday-things-revised-and-expanded-edition/).
