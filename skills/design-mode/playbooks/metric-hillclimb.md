### Metric hillclimb

**You own the metric and the experiment's integrity. One change per experiment, measured, then kept or reverted.**

Use it for sustained improvement of one UX metric. One reported problem goes to **UX defect fix** (playbooks/ux-defect-fix.md). The RITE method (Medlock et al. 2002) is the closest usability-lab analog of this loop. It was not researched, so it grounds nothing here.

1. Ground the metric in forensics. Read the funnel and analytics (tier 2) against the HEART plan from **Measure** (playbooks/measure.md). Find the step where users drop and the group it hits. With no HEART plan, run Measure's planning steps first. Choose HEART categories explicitly. The paper does not ask for all five (**principle-explain-the-metric**).
2. Fix one metric, the direction that counts as better, and a stop predicate that pairs a target with a floor on attempts, so a lucky early round cannot end the run. Use the user's numbers, otherwise propose labeled defaults and record them as open decisions (**principle-propose-and-proceed**).
3. Pre-register before the first change. Write down the overall evaluation criterion (one metric tied to long-term value), the guardrail metrics that must not get worse, and the baseline with its interval. Add the comparison (a concurrent control, or a pre/post window with its confounds such as seasonality and traffic mix stated), the minimum detectable effect, the practical-significance threshold, the required sample per arm, the run duration, and the stopping rule. Never peek at results before the stopping rule is met (**principle-explain-the-metric**).
4. Open a decision log, `decision.tsv`, with one row per experiment: id, hypothesis, design cause, change, before, after, difference with its interval, guardrails, verdict (kept, reverted, inconclusive, or pending), note.
5. Loop, one change per experiment. Ground each hypothesis in a named design cause, never "try a bigger button" (**principle-fix-the-design-cause**). Never stack untested changes.
6. Get the after number honestly. You cannot produce it for a live metric. It comes from analytics already flowing (tier 2) or from an experiment a human launches (tier 3). Shipping a variant to real users is outward, so pause for it. Until the number exists the verdict is `pending`. Never write an after value you did not read (**principle-never-fabricate-user-evidence**).
7. While a verdict is pending, the session delivers the pre-registered experiment, the ranked hypothesis backlog, and `decision.tsv`. Resume from `decision.tsv` when a result lands. Run independent hypotheses as separate concurrent experiments, never stacked in one.
8. Check integrity before reading any result. Compare the observed traffic split with the designed one. A sample ratio mismatch makes the result untrusted until you find the cause (about 6% of Microsoft experiments had one). Twyman's law says any figure that looks interesting or different is usually wrong. Double-check the data and rerun the validity checks before you believe a surprising effect (**principle-explain-the-metric**).
9. Decide on the interval of the difference, treatment minus control, or post minus pre with its confounds stated. Keep a change only when that interval excludes zero, clears the practical-significance threshold, and every guardrail holds. An interval that includes zero is inconclusive. Revert it and log it as inconclusive. Revert anything else in full. Log the row either way (**principle-prove-it-with-evidence**).
10. A run of reverts is normal. On a stall, diverge to a different category of hypothesis before you call the hill climbed (**principle-diverge-then-converge**). Stop at the predicate and never relax it to meet it.
11. Run **Design handoff** (playbooks/design-handoff.md) for the kept changes.

**Reply:** the metric, direction, and predicate, the pre-registration, baseline to final with intervals, experiments kept, reverted, inconclusive, and pending, open decisions, the integrity checks, the `decision.tsv` path, and the next hypothesis you would try.

**Grounded in:** [Rodden, Hutchinson and Fu: HEART (CHI 2010)](https://research.google/pubs/measuring-the-user-experience-on-a-large-scale-user-centered-metrics-for-web-applications/), [Kohavi, Tang and Xu: Trustworthy Online Controlled Experiments, ch. 1](https://experimentguide.com/wp-content/uploads/TrustworthyOnlineControlledExperiments_PracticalGuideToABTesting_Chapter1.pdf), [Fabijan et al.: Sample Ratio Mismatch (KDD 2019)](https://exp-platform.com/Documents/2019_KDDFabijanGupchupFuptaOmhoverVermeerDmitriev.pdf), [GOV.UK Service Manual: User research and measuring success](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research).
