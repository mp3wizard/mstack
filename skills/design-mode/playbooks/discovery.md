### Discovery

**You own the problem framing and a go or no-go recommendation, not a solution. The human owns the call.** Nothing gets built in discovery.

Use it for new work before any design exists. A question about an existing design goes to **Investigation** (playbooks/investigation.md).

1. Reframe the request as a user problem with **principle-frame-the-problem**. Write who has it, in what circumstance, and the progress they want (the Jobs to Be Done framing). A requested solution becomes one candidate, not the brief. Write what is out of scope.
2. Inventory the evidence by tier per the evidence-tier rule in **design-mode**. Tier 1 is what you can inspect now. Tier 2 is research, analytics, tickets, and recordings already in the project. Cite it and never extend it. Tier 3 is what only new research can answer. List it. No invented quotes, personas, or participants (**principle-never-fabricate-user-evidence**).
3. Pin the regimes before any design. The accessibility target and the legal reference that sets it. Gate on the pinned legal target and the current WCAG edition, with their current status in ../design-handbook/references/accessibility.md (**principle-accessible-by-default**). The ethics and privacy regime, including consent flows and children's services (**principle-honest-design**). Whether AI is involved. If it is, queue **AI feature design** (playbooks/ai-feature-design.md).
4. Map the users, the current journey, and the constraints (policy, legal, technical, budget). Pull method cards from 18F Discover and Decide where they fit, such as journey mapping or task flow analysis. List the options that need no build, such as better content, a process change, a partner, or open data (**principle-as-little-design-as-possible**).
5. Rank the assumptions by risk and by how little evidence backs them (**principle-riskiest-assumption-first**). Mark which tier could settle each one.
6. Define the outcome metric with HEART's goals, signals, metrics order (**principle-explain-the-metric**). Start from agreed goals, not from what is already logged. Choose the HEART categories explicitly. Tracking all five is not the rule.
7. If a top assumption needs tier 3, prepare the study kit. A question about needs, circumstances, or current behavior goes to **Interview study** (playbooks/interview-study.md). A question about whether people can use an existing design goes to **Usability test** (playbooks/usability-test.md). Record it as a pending gate and keep working on tiers 1 and 2.
8. Write the go or no-go memo with your recommendation, and record the call as an open decision for the human. Recommend go only if a problem worth solving exists and a viable route to it does. Carry forward the reframed problem, the ranked assumptions, the outcome metric, the regimes, and the pending gates. No-go is a legitimate result. A go routes to **Prototype** (playbooks/prototype.md) or **Feature design** (playbooks/feature-design.md).

**Reply:** the reframed problem, the evidence inventory by tier, the regimes, the no-build options, the ranked assumptions, the outcome metric, the pending gates, and the go or no-go recommendation with its reason as an open decision. Label every finding with its tier.

**Grounded in:** [GOV.UK discovery phase](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/agile-delivery/how-the-discovery-phase-works), [Design Council Framework for Innovation](https://www.designcouncil.org.uk/our-resources/framework-for-innovation/), [18F Methods](https://github.com/18F/guides/tree/main/content/methods), [Jobs to Be Done](https://www.christenseninstitute.org/theory/jobs-to-be-done/), [HEART (Rodden et al., CHI 2010)](https://research.google/pubs/measuring-the-user-experience-on-a-large-scale-user-centered-metrics-for-web-applications/).
