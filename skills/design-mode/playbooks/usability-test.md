### Usability test

**You own the study kit and the synthesis. A human runs the sessions. Never simulate a participant, a quote, a rating, or a task result, even labeled as simulated (principle-never-fabricate-user-evidence).**

Tier 3 evidence. Prepare the kit, hand it over, keep working on tier 1 meanwhile, and record the missing evidence as a pending gate in the todo list. This kit is evaluative. A question about needs, circumstances, or current behavior goes to **Interview study** (playbooks/interview-study.md).

1. Write the research questions. Name the decision this round informs and the result that would change it (**principle-frame-the-problem**). Draft the plan with design:user-research if installed, then check it against these steps.
2. Write the recruit spec. One screener per distinct user group. About 5 participants per round for one group, 3 to 4 each for two groups, at least 3 each for three or more. Plan several small rounds, not one big one. Include disabled and assistive-technology users. A benchmark number (success rate, time, SUS) needs about 40 participants, so a 5-person round reports scores as directional.
3. Write the tasks. Realistic goals the participant would have, hard enough to expose problems, worded without hinting at the answer. One success criterion per task.
4. Write the facilitator script and consent. A fixed intro that says the design is under test, not the person. A think-aloud request, neutral prompts, recording and data-handling consent. Sessions of 30 to 60 minutes, at most 6 a day, with gaps of at least 15 minutes and a note-taker per session. Link [Krug's test kit](https://sensible.com/download-files/), never repost it.
5. Write the observer sheet and instruments. Observers log the worst problems per session. SEQ (7-point ease) after each task, with observed success and time beside it, since ease ratings correlate with success but do not replace it. SUS at the end, or UMUX-Lite when 10 items is too many.
6. Write the analysis template: problem, task, participants affected (n of N), evidence (timestamp or clip), severity, proposed fix. Severity uses the 0 to 4 scale in ../design-handbook/references/heuristics.md and is rated in a separate pass once the problem list exists.
7. For information architecture, add a card-sort kit (cards from real content, about 15 participants per user group) and a tree-test kit for the drafted structure. The sort proposes a structure, the tree test checks it.
8. Dry-run every task on the prototype yourself, as the evaluator. Confirm each is completable and its wording gives no hint. Ask the human to pilot the kit with one colleague before recruiting.
9. Hand the kit over and keep working. Run **Heuristic review** (playbooks/heuristic-review.md) on the same tasks and label its output unvalidated until the round lands.
10. When a human returns real data, remove names and identifying details before analysis, and keep raw recordings only where the consent allows. Synthesize with design:research-synthesis if installed, else per the step 6 template, and check its output against these steps. Report the top problems with n of N, then rate severity (**principle-observe-behavior-over-opinion**). Score SUS with `scripts/sus.py` and report the mean with its interval. A SUS score is not a percentage (68 is about average, near the 50th percentile). Map UMUX-Lite through its published regression before comparing it to SUS norms. An SEQ mean below about 5.5 marks a harder-than-average task (**principle-explain-the-metric**).
11. Write the round report with the next round planned around what changed. Pause before sharing the report outside the session (see Autonomy in the router).

**Reply:** the research questions, the kit paths, the dry-run result, the pending gate, and after a round the top problems with n of N, severity, scores with intervals and their benchmark, and the next round's focus.

**Grounded in:** [GOV.UK Service Manual: User research](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research) (OGL v3.0, adapted), [NN/g: Why you only need to test with 5 users](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/why-you-only-need-to-test-with-5-users/), [NN/g: How many test users](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-many-test-users/) for 15 card-sort participants per user group, [NN/g: Card sorting, how many users](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/card-sorting-how-many-users-to-test/), [NN/g: Quantitative sample sizes](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/summary-quant-sample-sizes/) for about 40 benchmark participants, [MeasuringU: SUS](https://measuringu.com/sus/), [MeasuringU: SEQ](https://measuringu.com/evolution-of-seq/).
